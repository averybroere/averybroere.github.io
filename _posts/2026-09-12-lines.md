*Please note this is an opinions blog. It's light on sources, I just wanted to explain my thoughts.*

I have wanted to involve myself in some form of mass politics since I first started properly traveling as an adult and connecting to places outside my home. There is something inspiring about seeing a success story in another country, not just reading it but seeing, breathing, experiencing it. I remember first coming home from China in 2023, starry-eyed at their phenomenal domestic progress and wanting to be a part of something even a fraction that great. I was also returning in the wake of October 7th, the start of the greatest phase of the brutal genocide in Gaza, and hoped that we would take this rare moment of true reflection and horror to do *something*.

![Guangzhou](/assets/media/gz.jpg)

I visited the student encampment at UofT (which I fully endorse and exempt from my following criticisms), and I took the time to pick out some materials to start educating myself. However, that early motivation fell flat within months. I will take some credit and say I'm a bum, or a LARPer, if the shoe fits. I am at the beginning of a journey, not an expert. I do believe there is a broader reason for this fallout though, and I'm not alone in feeling this way from my conversations with others; our mass line is pathetic, and there is no movement in the first world truly setting an example to follow.

## The first world problem
This feels like a fairly basic concept to explain, but apparently the psychosis around this has spread far across the continent. Most people living in the first world are living at first world standards. Let's be serious here: our quality of life far eclipses 90% of the planet. Specifically, our absurdly boosted currency gives us purchasing power, and that power brings goods of all kinds. I would consider myself a low-income Canadian; I've never been able to afford my own place, purchase a car (a religious devotion here, as you might know), and I have no idea how I would ever retire as of now. The only time I saw serious savings in my bank account is when the government dumped a bunch of money on me during the pandemic. There are plenty of people in my situation.

And yet! I have multiple powerful internet-connected devices that make me capable of learning, producing and broadcasting my interests. I can go to school, not for free, but with nearly 0 upfront cost and a loan that is not by any means unconquerable. We all have a slurry of products finely engineered to boost our dopamine at any given moment; junk food, infinite short and long form *free* content from anywhere in the world, video games, the list goes on. Maybe we aren't the most healthy, but most of us eat enough calories. We have healthcare, of dwindling but acceptable quality. We are not sold, we are not peasants. You get my point.

This is all to say that first-worlders are by no means suffering in a way that causes mass mobilization. If you look at previous revolutionary movements, they are anchored to an underclass, a people that are being screwed down in place to their poverty, to serfdom, or to exploitation. Exploitation certainly still exists here, but we have gotten very good at not only offshoring it elsewhere, but bringing formerly targeted groups into the folds of privilege, to help us reinforce a sort of skewed equality. So, with this in mind, who do our movements speak to?

## Social Democracy has gotten us nowhere
As Canada is being targeted by our former partner in crime the USA, our PM Carney is ramping up rhetoric about a future of "middle powers", aka an alliance between ourselves and the EU, the champions of so-called Social Democracy. There is also the rise of the DSA, America's own SocDem party that is gaining steam. To be blunt, they are the true weasels of the 21st century, simply a better looking, smooth talking group of cloak-and-dagger neoliberals who are going to stab you in the back and leave you to die if you trust them.

For one, they are ineffective at their spoken goal of promoting a Social Democracy, a socialism with no socialist characteristics beyond straight improvement, supposedly softer and more "democratic", at least purely in a parliamentary, status quo sense. They are selling slightly improved working conditions to people that, on average, are content with their status and lot in life, without any of the hardship or societal transformation that a revolutionary movement entails. This is a fantasy that relies on ignoring *how* we've achieved our enormous wealth (imperialism), and what it would take to maintain it, and they have no intentions of revealing their hand on this issue.

Even worse than this, they capitulate and give in to their liberal tendencies far more than they'd like to admit. In theory, this system is still a lesser evil to what we have, but they are *liars*, with no backbone or willingness to commit. Our NDP, long proposed as an alternative to the effective duopoly here, will side with the liberals long before they listen to the people time and again. They did it with their "alliance" during the Trudeau years, where they pushed for barely anything and then called the whole thing off, and they continue to do it now by sidelining Palestine and giving wishy-washy two state solution pleas, perfectly in line with the ruling party. They are not even willing to fully endorse the eradication of for-profit housing, a policy so blatantly popular and useful you could get a hardline conservative to agree if framed correctly. Instead, they support half-measures of percentages and quota increases, nonsense that can be instantly reversed after they inevitably lose power.

The DSA is by far the worst in both these regards, featuring a monstrous internal bureaucracy on the level of the Soviets, but with the power of a local council. There are committees to elect committees to appoint committees all the way down, and major decisions are made in 3-year cycles, ensuring that absolutely nothing is decided in good time. Nothing, of course, except voting for Democrats! Yes, naturally, since they're also split into dozens upon dozens of caucuses with opposing beliefs, all the major ones can simply sidestep this process of deciding the party line and advocate for you to conveniently follow the existing system. You're doing your part as a revolutionary by participating in our circus, and your part as a citizen by voting for the genocidal maniacs (the nice ones, who we are suspiciously palatable to). This is not a serious organization.

What of the Europeans? I am less informed on this issue, but is the rise of the AfD in Germany, or Meloni in Italy, or Orban in Hungary, not a sign that cracks are forming? What about France or the UK, who despite their willingness to take to the streets have been endlessly broiled in neoliberal controversy after controversy? They are horrified at the prospect of losing their combined power, and turn more reactionary by the day. Where is this so-called Social Democracy shining?

## Existing Socialists in the first world
An alternative title for this section would be "The Newspaper Salesmen".

![Baltimore Maoists](/assets/media/baltimore-maoist.png)
<small>A cryptid-style sighting of the famous Baltimore Maoists.</small>

I will give the existing socialist/communist parties here a little more credit and say that sometimes, they do the right thing. They organize (or at least appear) at important protests. Some, like the YCL, will arrange aid and work trips to Cuba (and unlike that flotilla, the people pay their own way). They are less likely to randomly endorse liberals, and they are understandably very small groups with only a little bit of power, operating in countries that have bludgeoned over 50 years of cold war fear into our consciousness. All well and good.

This does not exempt them from their own proprietary brand of ineffectiveness. While the SocDems have adapted to the times, more effectively camouflaging their language and intentions, the socialists continue to pretend as if they're still in the 20th century. They speak even stronger about liberating us, but from what chains, exactly? They are too busy condemning Stalin and upholding Trotsky (names that barely enter our cultural space outside brief mentions in history classes) to answer that question. They are too busy fervently selling their circlejerk newspapers, fighting with anarchists on Twitter, or implementing the *true* revolutionary line, Socialism in One Polycule, to answer any questions really.

Forgive my jabs, I honestly still consider all these groups better allies than the NDP. It is just to point out how unserious, how insular this all has become. There is no "mass" to this mass movement, it is a series of competing clubs, social media feeds and newsletters bickering over ancient squabbles. When you look at their actual platforms, at their calls to action, it feels so *old*. They are still mostly calling to improve domestic working conditions, but a little more red, with a smattering of affiliations/disavowals depending on flavor. If there is one thing I'm begging anyone reading to take away from this, it's this: you cannot motivate privileged people with promises of more privilege. This playbook is absolutely unfit for our material conditions.

## Is there a solution?
I would not be so presumptuous to say I've figured it out. I have thoughts, of course. I do *not* believe in some sort of "Left Unity", because I don't want to unify with a fake left coming from the liberal sphere, and historically in leftist governments someone has to "take the reigns", to prove themselves the most sturdy and set the national line. What I do think is worthwhile is setting myself a baseline, a simple set of principles that I think are better suited to what's actually important here, ceasing imperialism. They are as follows:

1. **Foster relationships with AES countries and existing movements**: This one is already too much for many of these groups, but I'm sorry, you can't "win" geopolitics alone. Stop condemning everything and acting like you have nothing to learn. A revisionist China, or hell even the current DPRK are far better allies than the EU or your local liberals who will happily suffocate you once you cross their red lines; they are more interested in absorbing your energy for themselves. Plus, there are movements in places like LATAM that are more mobilized than we are, and we could learn from them.
2. **Ally with indigenous resistance**: This is another obvious one that existing groups just don't seem to do enough for. Not just "recognize the treaties", as this is meaningless idpol. Indigenous groups, particularly in rural Canada, are the closest thing we have to militant resistance. They live far below regular living standards (often without clean water barely an hour or two outside major areas), the RCMP are violently suppressing them, and there are people still of this generation who experienced an active genocide via the residential school system. Now, indigenous movements are just as prone to reactionary tendencies and overt liberalization as anything else, so why not get there first and supplant yourself as their shields, earning the support of the clearest-cut group that needs to be liberated?
3. **Replace SocDem-style focus on unions with a fierce opposition to our two-tiered labor system**: I've hopefully made my case as to why I think focusing so much on domestic labor for people who already have it pretty good is a pointless and losing strategy. There are groups supplying Canadian labor who *are* horrifically mistreated though: desperate immigrants. Recent arrivals often go through the LMIA system for example, which allows companies to pay well under a normal wage, and make their visas conditional on this employment, essentially cutting off upwards mobility. Many of them are not even lucky enough to receive this treatment, only "employed" via gig work. They do not have access to our healthcare. Then to top it all off, they are blamed for every problem and subjected to constant racist attacks and further restrictions. Now, you might say, isn't that just how immigration works? Not really; in some countries, like the *allegedly* most racist country ever Argentina, after living there for a short time you get all the benefits of a regular citizen even without citizenship, and it's constitutionally protected. Sounds a lot less racist than what we have.
4. **Demand an end to our international imperialism**: Many already do this, but it's in direct conflict with the domestic policies that promise only upward trajectory. There is simply no way to end our constant exploitation in say, the mining sector, without a noticeable drop in mined materials domestically. I understand it's hard to bring people on board with lowering their own QoL somewhat, but it *has* to be done, the industries and social fabric around them must be struck down and rebuilt anew without these gross ties to colonialism and imperialism. This will inevitably lower the rate of consumption, it will make the line go down, but the future at the other end is worth it.
5. **Dissolve the national identity**: Building off point 4, I simply do not believe it is possible to maintain this nationalistic first-world identity without also maintaining its imperialism, yet many first-worlders will paradoxically say they are a leftist and a patriot. You should not be "proud" to be a member of a country that inflicts infinite suffering outside its borders. Frankly, can you not think a little bigger? Imagine if the USSR had kept elements of its tsarist identity and iconography. Imagine if China still called itself a dynasty. What would an actually socialist North America look like? Would there even be separated countries? Would it eventually be a union between the US, Canada, and Mexico? This transition should not be seamless. You strip the disturbing pieces of your country's flesh clean off and write a new history, as the previous success stories did before you.

The goal of these 5 points is to move away from this idea of mobilizing our unmotivated, reformist core populations, and towards supporting international movements in states that still have the capacity to seriously change. In the process, the first world's grip weakens, our perverted nationalism weakens, and the potential for transformation here also increases. The chance of some sort of domestic revolution in the first world under our current conditions has been growing slimmer since the fallout of the Secret Speech and the individualization/liberalization of the anti-war movements all the way back in the 1960s. It's time to update the playbook and put the effort somewhere more useful.

## Conclusion
I've been thinking lately about the call Hugo Chavez made in the 2000s pleading for a 5th International. That must have briefly felt quite hopeful. Yet you look at a large group's response at the time, like the RCP, and they spend half their winding article... condemning Stalin, again, for what was likely the 100th time, before proudly announcing all *30* of your committee have *voted* to support this new international! Christ. Is this the sum total of our progress? Blogs and self-aggrandized councils?

I hope this all made some sense. Perhaps one day I can present these ideas again, in a way that will influence others for the better.
